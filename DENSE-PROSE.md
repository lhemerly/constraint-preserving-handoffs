# Dense prose and structured CPH

The originally observed style was dense single-paragraph technical prose: telegraphic clauses, omitted articles/connectors, chained scope and stop conditions, shared artifact references, compact status wording and sometimes selective word fusion. It was not a labeled-field schema. Structured CPH is a later designed variant and the recommended default.

Selective fusions above expand familiar terms. They do not justify omitting action verbs, permission boundaries, uncertainty, provenance or stop conditions. Critical instructions remain explicit. Switch to structured fields when compact wording is harder to review.

The experiment tested a composite style: layout, telegraphic wording, connector compression and selective fusion. It cannot attribute results to whitespace alone. No specific training origin, emergent language or novelty over structured prompting is established. See [exact frozen dense bodies and expansions](benchmarks/v2.2/DENSE-EXPANSIONS.md).
